For the video guide avatar. Last updated November 9, 2026.

=====================================================================
A. STEP-BY-STEP WALKTHROUGHS (what to say and where to click)
=====================================================================

A1. RESEARCH A COMPANY
1. Click "Open Research" in the main switch (or the Research Hub logo).
2. Type the company and what you want to know, for example
   "Stripe latest funding round and investors".
3. Optional: pick sources in the source row. Leave "Curated sources" and
   "Web" on for most questions. Click "All" to search everything.
4. Optional: set "Published" to "Past year" for recent news only.
5. Press Enter or click "Search".
6. Read the Summary first, then check the claim-check figures and the
   numbered sources. Click a [Source n] number to open that source.
7. Scroll to "Your network and Stripe" to see who you know there.

A2. CHECK GOVERNMENT, PATENT OR RESEARCH SOURCES
1. On Open Research, switch on "Gov & regulators" for filings, enforcement
   actions, healthcare (CMS, FDA), banking (Federal Reserve, FDIC, OCC) and
   state business registries.
2. Switch on "Patents & IP" for patents, trademarks, copyright and research
   grants (USPTO, Google Patents, WIPO, DARPA, NSF, NIH, SBIR).
3. Switch on "Institutions" for UN, World Bank, IMF, OECD, consulting-firm
   reports (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Big Four) and think tanks (Brookings, RAND).
4. Search as usual. Government and patent results are labelled
   "Filing / official"; institutional reports are labelled as reputable.

A3. RESEARCH WITH YOUR OWN DOCUMENTS OR LINKS
1. On Open Research, click "Custom" at the end of the source row.
2. Paste a web address and add it, or upload up to 5 files
   (PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT, 25 MB each).
3. Search. Your files and links are searched together with other sources.

A4. RUN A FOUNDER CHECK
1. Click "Founder Check" in the main switch.
2. Type the founder's full name, for example "Arthur Mensch".
3. Optional: add the company, for example "Mistral", to avoid mix-ups with
   people of the same name.
4. Click "Run check".
5. Read the brief. Check the disambiguation warning if it appears.
6. Scroll to "Who can help diligence <name>" for reference calls.

A5. IMPORT YOUR LINKEDIN CONNECTIONS
1. On LinkedIn: Settings & Privacy > Data privacy > Get a copy of your data.
2. Choose "Connections" and request the archive. LinkedIn emails a download
   link, usually within minutes.
3. Unzip the download and find "Connections.csv".
4. On Research Hub, open "Network Map".
5. In the Sources card, click "Upload Connections.csv" on the LinkedIn card
   (or drag the file onto the card).
6. The map appears with your contacts grouped by role.
Tip: sign in first so the contacts are saved to your account.

A6. IMPORT A SPREADSHEET OF CONTACTS
1. Make sure the file has a header row with a name column ("Name", or
   "First Name" and "Last Name") and ideally "Company", "Title", "Email",
   "LinkedIn URL", "Location" and "Tags".
2. Click "Upload contacts" on the CSV / XLSX card.
Note: a new upload replaces the previous upload of the same type.

A7. FIND WHO CAN HELP YOU RAISE A FUND
1. On Network Map, click into the search bar and pick
   "Who can help me raise a fund?", or type your own question.
2. Results list LPs and family offices first, then investors and bankers,
   each with a role label and the reason they matched.
3. Or click the "raise capital" chip above the map to show only those people.

A8. FIND DEAL FLOW IN A SECTOR
1. Ask "Who sends me deal flow in fintech?" (replace fintech with any
   sector).
2. Founders in that sector come first, then investors and operators.

A9. FIND WHO CAN DILIGENCE A COMPANY OR SECTOR
1. Ask "Who can diligence an AI company?" or "Who can do reference checks?".
2. Or click the "diligence" chip above the map.
3. Or open the company's profile or run a Founder Check and use the
   "Your network" panel there.

A10. FIND INTRO PATHS TO A COMPANY
1. Ask "Intro paths to Stripe".
2. Results show: people who work there (direct), people at similar names
   (possibly related), and people at that company's investors.

A11. EXPLORE THE MAP LAYER BY LAYER
1. Choose a grouping: "By role" (default), "By company", "By industry" or
   "By country".
2. Click a group on the map, or its name in the navigator bar at the bottom.
3. The group's people are regrouped by the next level (for example by
   industry). Click again to reach companies and people.
4. Click a company to see the people you know there.
5. Use the breadcrumb ("All > ...") to go back up.
6. "Full screen" enlarges the map; "Fit to view" re-centres it.
7. Scroll on the map to zoom; drag to move around.

A12. CREATE A DEAL WORKSPACE AND MEMO
1. Sign in. Click "Workspaces" (top right).
2. Create a workspace with the deal name and company.
3. On Open Research, set "Save to" to that workspace before searching.
4. In the workspace, add notes, pin sources and upload data-room documents.
5. Ask questions in the data room; answers cite [D1], [D2]...
6. Open "Memo" and download it as Markdown or Word.

A13. WATCH A COMPANY
1. Sign in. Click "Watchlist" (top right).
2. Type the company name with its exact capitalisation (for example "Ramp")
   and click "Watch company".
3. Alerts appear on the page; "Check now" runs a check immediately.

=====================================================================
B. FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
=====================================================================

Q: Is the answer made up by AI?
A: No. The answer is written only from the sources shown, and every
   sentence carries a [Source n] citation. When no AI is available, the
   answer is made of direct quotes and is labelled "Quoted from sources".

Q: What does "corroborated", "single-source" and "conflicting" mean?
A: For funding and valuation figures: corroborated means two or more
   different websites agree; single-source means only one site says it;
   conflicting means sources disagree, so check them before relying on it.

Q: What do the source quality labels mean?
A: Filing / official (government and regulator records) ranks highest,
   then Reputable press, then Blog / newsletter, then Forum / social.

Q: Are my contacts private?
A: Yes. They are stored only in your account, shown only to you, and never
   sent to an AI model or any other service. "Remove all" deletes them.

Q: Why does "Connect LinkedIn" not import my connections?
A: LinkedIn does not give any app access to a user's connections. The only
   way to bring them in is LinkedIn's own data export (Connections.csv).

Q: What does Connect X do?
A: It signs you in with X and can import the accounts you follow, so they
   appear on your map. The import depends on the site owner's X API plan.

Q: What is a "firm contact" or a hollow dot?
A: Someone known through a colleague in the firm's CRM rather than your own
   direct connection. It is labelled "Firm via <colleague>". The
   "my direct connections only" chip hides them. (CRM import is coming soon.)

Q: Why is someone under "Everyone else" or "Other"?
A: Their title and company do not say what they do or what industry they
   are in (for example a blank title, or a small unknown company). LinkedIn
   exports have no location, so "By country" shows them under "Unknown"
   unless a spreadsheet with locations is uploaded.

Q: Can I get my second-degree connections?
A: Not from LinkedIn; its export contains only first-degree connections.

Q: Do I need an account?
A: No for research, Founder Check and the map. Yes to save history,
   workspaces, watchlists and to keep contacts between visits.

Q: What file types can I upload?
A: Research and data room: PDF, DOCX, CSV, TXT. Network Map: CSV, XLSX,
   and X following export (JSON).

=====================================================================
C. LIMITS AND WHAT TO SAY WHEN SOMETHING IS MISSING
=====================================================================

- If a source is unavailable, a banner names the skipped sources and the
  search continues with the rest.
- "Not enabled on this deployment" means that capability is switched off on
  this site, not that the user did something wrong.
- A scanned PDF without a text layer cannot be read; other files in the same
  upload still work.
- Contact limits: 30,000 contacts per account, 25 MB per file.
- Large networks (over 1,500 people) show companies first; click a company
  to see its people.
- Founder Check needs a full name (first and last).
- The Watchlist matches company names with exact capitalisation.
- Output is information, not investment, legal or professional advice.
  Always check the cited sources before relying on anything.

=====================================================================
D. TONE AND WORDING FOR THE AVATAR
=====================================================================

- Speak like a calm, precise analyst: short sentences, concrete steps,
  name the exact button or chip to click.
- Use the exact on-screen names: "Open Research", "Network Map",
  "Founder Check", "Search", "All", "Custom", "Gov & regulators",
  "Patents & IP", "Institutions", "Upload Connections.csv", "Connect X",
  "By role", "Full screen", "Fit to view", "raise capital",
  "source deals", "diligence".
- Do not mention pricing, API keys or technical settings to users.
- When unsure whether a feature is available, say "If you see ... on your
  screen, click it; if not, it is not enabled on this site yet."
